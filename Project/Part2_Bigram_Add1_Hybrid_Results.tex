\subsubsection{Experimental Results}

The edited system augments the original TF-IDF cosine score with a phrase-aware bigram bonus using bigrams of the form \texttt{boundary\_layer} and an interpolation weight of $0.1$.
Evaluation metrics were computed for values of $k$ ranging from 1 to 10.

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was conducted separately for:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Precision@k
    \item Recall@k
    \item F$_{0.5}$@k
\end{itemize}

for all values of $k$ from 1 to 10.\\

The null hypothesis used was:

H0: \texttt{There is no statistically significant difference between the baseline TF-IDF system and the TF-IDF + add-one-smoothed bigram hybrid retrieval system.}

The alternative hypothesis was:

H1: \texttt{The TF-IDF + add-one-smoothed bigram hybrid system produces statistically significant retrieval improvements over the baseline TF-IDF system.}\\

A significance threshold of:

\[
\alpha = 0.05
\]

was used.

The edited model improves early retrieval quality numerically over the baseline, with gains in Precision@1 through Precision@4, stronger MAP at every reported $k$, and higher nDCG across the full range of $k$.
The strongest absolute gains are concentrated in the early ranks, where phrase-aware matching helps the system reward local contextual patterns that plain unigram TF-IDF misses.

However, none of the Wilcoxon p-values fall below $0.05$.
This means the observed improvements are not statistically significant at the chosen threshold, even though the trend is favorable for early precision, MAP, and nDCG.

Overall, the add-one-smoothed bigram hybrid improves lexical ranking behavior without introducing a strong statistical separation from the baseline TF-IDF system on Cranfield.
The results suggest that phrase-aware evidence is useful, but a stronger gain will likely require additional ranking improvements beyond local bigram context alone.
